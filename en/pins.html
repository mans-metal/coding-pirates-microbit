Pins: which one do I use?

Use this when you are plugging in a part and wondering which pin to use.

What it is

A pin is a metal contact where the micro:bit talks to the outside world. Each pin has a number. Most pins are identical: any free pin can switch a light on or off. To read a range, like a knob, use pin 0, 1 or 2. The only thing that matters is that the pin number in your code is the pin you plugged into. Plug an LED into pin 8 and your block must say pin 8.

On the breakout board the plugs carry the same numbers, 0 to 16, so the number on the board is the number in your code.

Two kinds of jobs

  • On or off (digital): the pin reads or writes just 0 or 1 (0 = off, 1 = on). Almost every pin can do this. Examples: an LED brick, the PIR sensor, the crash sensor.
  • A range of values (analog): the pin reads a number from 0 to 1023, like a dimmer knob turned from low to high. Examples: the potentiometer, the soil moisture sensor. Only some pins can do this.

Which pins are special?

PinsWhat they areWhat to do
0, 1, 2Free pins that can do on/off and read a range of values.Your best choice, especially for sensors with a range.
8, 13, 14, 15, 16Free pins for on/off.Good for LEDs, the PIR sensor and the crash sensor.
3, 4, 6, 7, 10 (and 9 on a V1)The lights use these pins too.Avoid them while you use the lights. Your part may act strangely or the lights may flicker.
5, 11Buttons A and B.Avoid them, or your part will press the buttons for you.
19, 20The plug for the OLED screen (called I2C).Leave them for it.
12The micro:bit keeps it for itself.Skip it.

If a pin acts strangely, move your part to a different one.

Common mistakes

  • My part does nothing. The pin in the block is not the pin you plugged into.
  • The lights flicker when I plug something in. You used a pin that the lights use too (3, 4, 6, 7 or 10, and 9 on a V1). Move to another pin.
  • My sensor reading does not change. Check the pin number first. A sensor that gives a range works best on pin 0, 1 or 2.

The code in words

You choose the pin in the drop-down of the block.

JavaScript:  pins.digitalWritePin(DigitalPin.P8, 1)
             pins.analogReadPin(AnalogPin.P1)

Python:      pins.digital_write_pin(DigitalPin.P8, 1)
             pins.analog_read_pin(AnalogPin.P1)

V1 and V2

The table works for both. On a V2, pin 9 is free too, because the V2 lights do not use it. If a pin acts strangely, look at the pin list for your board in MakeCode.

Teacher notes
